# Gate 0 — Risk Register

Prioritized by (impact × likelihood), highest first. "Owner" is who needs to
act, not necessarily who's blamed. This list is a living document — update it
at every gate, per Section 47's per-milestone reporting requirement.

| # | Risk | Impact | Likelihood | Priority | Mitigation | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R-1 | AI-generated quest recommends or publishes a factually wrong or unsafe activity (bad hours, private property presented as public, unsafe trail) that a user acts on | Severe — physical safety, legal exposure, trust collapse | Medium (inherent to LLM generation without strict grounding) | Critical | Enforce QB-064/QB-070/QB-072 as hard gates in code (feasibility evaluator is mandatory, not advisory); never let Quest Forge output bypass the feasibility pipeline even for "just preview" UI; maintain the AI eval suite (QB-266) as a release gate | Product + Eng |
| R-2 | Minor accesses age-restricted (alcohol/gambling/adult) content or an adult contacts a minor through party/social features | Severe — legal (COPPA-adjacent), reputational, child-safety incident | Medium | Critical | Age gating enforced server-side on every query path, not just UI; no open DM in MVP (already excluded, Section 5); child profile flag checked in the same hard-filter pass as legality (Section 12 ranking order already models this) | Product + Eng + Legal |
| R-3 | Google Places/Maps terms are violated (e.g., persisting place data beyond allowed cache windows, mixing basemap providers, missing attribution) — risk of API key suspension or legal claim | Severe — could break core discovery entirely if key is revoked | Medium (terms are detailed and easy to violate accidentally) | Critical | Read current ToS before Gate 1 data model is finalized (Section 32 already requires this); build the Places adapter to expire/refresh cached fields, never treat a Place ID snapshot as permanently owned data; see `04-provider-licensing-questions.md` | Eng + Legal |
| R-4 | Prompt injection via untrusted external content (place reviews, flyers, user-submitted text) manipulates AI generation or moderation output | High — could produce unsafe/offensive/fraudulent quests at scale | Medium | High | Enforce QB-263 architecturally: external text always enters as *data* in a clearly separated context field, never concatenated into system/instruction prompts; include injection cases in the AI eval suite (QB-266) | Eng |
| R-5 | Geographic sparse-data areas produce a broken or embarrassing experience (empty map, fabricated-sounding suggestions) outside Massachusetts pilot | Medium-High — poor first impression, but not safety-critical if fallbacks work | High (most of the US is sparse relative to Boston-level density) | High | Sparse-data fallback behavior (QB-231) must ship in R1, not be an afterthought; geographic readiness score (QB-232) should gate where the app *encourages* discovery vs. just tolerates it | Product + Eng |
| R-6 | Fraudulent business venue claims or fake rewards (Section 25 lists this explicitly as a reportable incident) | Medium-High — financial/trust harm to real businesses and users | Low in R1 (business platform is R2), rising after launch | Medium (until R2), High (at R2) | Verification workflow (Section 41) required before any claim goes live; this is correctly scoped to Release 2 already — don't pull business claims earlier without the verification admin tooling | Product + Eng |
| R-7 | GPS spoofing / impossible-travel abuse of fog-of-war or geofenced completion inflates rewards or fakes exploration | Medium — economy integrity, not safety | Medium | Medium | Anomaly detection per QB-122, but must be tuned to *never* penalize legitimate GPS drift, transit, or accessibility accommodations — false positives are their own harm (Section 20 explicit warning) | Eng |
| R-8 | Team builds a big-bang release instead of the mandated vertical-slice gates (Sections 47-48), producing a large unreviewable diff and drifting from the decision hierarchy under time pressure | Medium — process/quality risk, not user-facing | Medium-High (natural pressure on any ambitious spec like this) | High | Treat Gates 0-7 as literal stop points requiring sign-off, as this document does; each gate's report must include known limitations and deferred work per Section 47, so drift is visible early | Product + Eng |
| R-9 | AI/provider cost overrun (Places calls, LLM generation calls, weather/events APIs) at scale with no budget enforcement | Medium — financial, could force a feature rollback post-launch | Medium | Medium | Section 40's requirement for "defined per-quest AI and provider cost budgets before public scale" must be a Gate 1/2 deliverable with actual numbers, not deferred indefinitely; nearby-generator's "variants on demand rather than storing every combination" (QB-063) directly trades off cache cost vs. compute cost — needs a real budget model | Product + Eng |
| R-10 | Reused `questmap` code silently reintroduces spec-violating patterns (Mapbox instead of Google Places as primary, Stripe payment flows the spec defers to R2, no accessibility-confidence modeling, different age/child rules) | Medium — technical debt and spec drift disguised as "reuse for speed" | Medium (temptation to copy working code) | Medium | Any code ported from `questmap` gets an explicit compliance check against this traceability matrix before merging — see `07-prior-art-questmap.md` | Eng |
| R-11 | Accessibility "unknown" state gets silently treated as "accessible" somewhere in the stack (e.g., a default `true` boolean, a missing-field-means-yes filter) | Medium-High — directly harms disabled users who rely on accurate data (Principle 10) | Medium (a very common default-value bug pattern) | High | Model accessibility fields as an explicit 5-state enum with no boolean shortcuts anywhere in schema or filters (QB-191); add a specific test asserting "unknown" never satisfies an "accessible" filter | Eng |
| R-12 | Sponsored/business ranking boost leaks into organic quality signals over time (rating manipulation, hidden criticism) despite stated policy | Medium — regulatory (FTC disclosure) and trust risk | Low in R1 (feature not built yet), rising at R2 | Medium (deferred) | Enforce the "gate first, boost after, always labeled" ranking rule (QB-053, QB-250) in the ranking algorithm's structure itself, not as a policy layered on top late | Product + Eng |
| R-13 | Legal ambiguity on independent-minor age threshold (13 vs 16) and jurisdiction-specific age-restricted content (drinking/gambling ages) shipped with a guessed default | Medium-High — legal/compliance exposure if wrong | Medium until resolved | High until DL-002 is answered | Do not hardcode an age default in engineering; keep it config-driven per jurisdiction and get explicit legal sign-off before launch (see decision log DL-002) | Legal + Product |

## Categories not yet risk-scored (need more information before Gate 1)

- Insurance/liability exposure for real-world activity recommendations
  (hiking, extreme sports guilds) — this is a legal-entity question tracked
  in the decision log (DL-001), not an engineering risk per se.
- Moderation staffing capacity vs. expected submission volume at public
  launch — operational risk, tracked as DL-009.
